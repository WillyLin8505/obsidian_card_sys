# Thinking Model Skill — AQAL Model (All Quadrants, All Levels)

> **Orchestrator Routing Trigger (Logic Gate F):** Awaken this Skill when a technically correct solution fails to gain adoption, when organizational change stalls, when a product solves the functional need but users still reject it, or when stakeholders are talking past each other because they are operating from different quadrants.

---

## Layer 1: Core Definition

An integral framework developed by Ken Wilber that insists every complete analysis must account for **All Quadrants** (the four irreducible perspectives on any phenomenon) and **All Levels** (the developmental stage at which individuals and collectives are operating). Neglecting any quadrant produces partial solutions that fail in the neglected dimension.

**Output feeds into**: Six Thinking Hats (quadrant findings map to specific hats), or Iceberg Model (Lower-Left quadrant = shared mental models = deepest iceberg layer).

---

## Layer 2: Master's Mindset

**Chief Practitioner**: Ken Wilber (*A Brief History of Everything*, *Integral Psychology*).

### The Four Quadrants

|  | **Interior** (Subjective) | **Exterior** (Objective) |
|--|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Individual** | **Upper-Left (UL)** — "I" — Consciousness, emotions, intention, meaning, belief | **Upper-Right (UR)** — "It" — Behavior, brain states, measurable performance, physiology |
| **Collective** | **Lower-Left (LL)** — "We" — Culture, shared values, worldview, language, norms | **Lower-Right (LR)** — "Its" — Systems, institutions, processes, infrastructure, markets |

### The Levels (Abbreviated)

Each quadrant contains developmental levels — from less complex to more complex:

| Level | Description | Implication |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Archaic / Survival | Pure survival instinct | Rarely relevant in modern orgs |
| Tribal / Safety | Group belonging, tradition | Resistance to change from identity threat |
| Power | Assertive, results-driven | Responds to authority and competition |
| Conformist | Rule-following, conventional | Needs process clarity and role definition |
| Achievement | Rational, merit-based | Responds to data, efficiency, outcomes |
| Pluralistic | Inclusive, values-driven | Responds to meaning, fairness, participation |
| Integral | Systems-aware, quadrant-literate | Can hold multiple perspectives simultaneously |

**Key Insight**: A solution designed at the Achievement level will be rejected by users operating at the Conformist level who need rules, not options. Match the intervention to the level, not just the quadrant.

## Layer 3: Step-by-Step Execution

**All four quadrants are mandatory. Level assessment is required for UL and LL.**

### Step 1: Frame the Focus Question

**Action**: State precisely what is being analyzed — a decision, a product, a change initiative, an organizational failure.

**Self-Prompt**: *What outcome are we trying to produce or understand? What is the unit of analysis (individual, team, organization, market)?*

---

### Step 2: Upper-Left (UL) — "I" — Individual Interior

**Action**: Map the interior experience of the key individual(s) involved. What do they believe, feel, value, and intend? What meaning do they make of this situation?

**Self-Prompt**: *What is the user/stakeholder/leader actually thinking and feeling — not what they report publicly, but what drives their behavior? What does this situation mean to them?*

> This is the most frequently skipped quadrant in technical and business analysis. Skipping it produces solutions that are logically sound but psychologically unacceptable.

---

### Step 3: Upper-Right (UR) — "It" — Individual Exterior

**Action**: Map observable, measurable individual behaviors and outputs. What metrics capture individual performance? What behaviors are visible and trackable?

**Self-Prompt**: *What does success look like in measurable terms for an individual? What behaviors do we want to increase or decrease? What does the data show?*

---

### Step 4: Lower-Left (LL) — "We" — Collective Interior

**Action**: Map the shared culture, values, norms, and language of the relevant group. What is the unspoken agreement about "how we do things here"? What worldview does the group share?

**Self-Prompt**: *What cultural assumptions does this group hold? What will they automatically resist because it violates their shared values? What narrative does this group use to explain itself to itself?*

> The Lower-Left is where organizational change dies. A technically perfect solution introduced in violation of cultural norms will be quietly killed by the collective.

---

### Step 5: Lower-Right (LR) — "Its" — Collective Exterior

**Action**: Map the systems, structures, processes, institutions, and incentives that govern collective behavior. What does the org chart show? What do the incentive structures reward? What infrastructure constrains options?

**Self-Prompt**: *What systemic structures currently produce the outcomes we are observing? What would need to change in the infrastructure, process, or incentive system to produce different collective behavior?*

---

### Step 6: Level Assessment

**Action**: For UL and LL, estimate the dominant developmental level currently operating. Then assess: what level does the proposed solution assume?

**Self-Prompt**: *Is there a level mismatch? If our solution requires Pluralistic-level values but our users are operating from Conformist-level needs, what bridge do we need to build?*

---

### Step 7: Synthesis — Identify the Neglected Quadrant

**Action**: Identify which quadrant(s) have received the least attention in prior attempts to address this problem. That neglected quadrant is almost always where the solution has failed.

## Layer 4: Output Specification

All outputs **must** strictly follow these three mandatory formats.

### Mandatory Format 1 — Four-Quadrant Map

|  | Interior | Exterior |
|--|----------|---------|
| **Individual** | **UL — "I"**: *(key beliefs, emotions, intentions of key individuals)* | **UR — "It"**: *(measurable behaviors, performance metrics, observable data)* |
| **Collective** | **LL — "We"**: *(shared culture, values, norms, resistance points)* | **LR — "Its"**: *(systems, processes, incentives, structural constraints)* |

### Mandatory Format 2 — Level Mismatch Assessment

| Dimension | Current Level | Solution Assumes Level | Mismatch? |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|---------|
| Individual (UL) | *(e.g., Conformist)* | *(e.g., Achievement)* | Yes / No |
| Collective (LL) | *(e.g., Tribal)* | *(e.g., Pluralistic)* | Yes / No |

### Mandatory Format 3 — Neglected Quadrant Intervention

Identify the single most neglected quadrant. Propose one specific intervention that addresses it.

> Example: *"The LR quadrant (incentive structure) has been ignored — the bonus system still rewards individual speed, undermining the team collaboration the solution requires. Corrective action: redesign the Q3 team bonus metric to reward collective cycle time reduction."*

## Layer 5: Pitfall Guide & Iteration Log

### Known Blind Spot 1 — Treating AQAL as a Checklist

The quadrants are not boxes to tick — they are lenses that reveal different real aspects of the same situation. **Analyze all four with equal seriousness, not as a compliance exercise.**

### Known Blind Spot 2 — Skipping Level Assessment

Filling the quadrant map without assessing developmental levels produces a flat analysis that misses the most common reason change initiatives fail: level mismatch between the solution and the audience.

### Known Blind Spot 3 — Confusing Quadrants

Interior ≠ soft/unimportant. Exterior ≠ real/important. Both interior and exterior are equally real — they are simply different aspects of the same phenomenon. **Resisting the UL and LL quadrants because they "can't be measured" is itself a level bias (Achievement-level scientism).**
