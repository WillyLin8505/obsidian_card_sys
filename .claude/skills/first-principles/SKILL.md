# Thinking Model Skill — First Principles

> **Orchestrator Routing Trigger (Logic Gate A):** Awaken this Skill when the task is noisy, deadlocked, or requires building a solution from scratch.

---

## Layer 1: Core Definition

A hardcore cognitive skill that forces the stripping away of complex problems down to indivisible **absolute physical or mathematical facts** — entirely discarding analogical thinking, historical experience, and industry consensus — to reconstruct solutions from the foundational level.

**Output feeds into**: SWOT Analysis (for sociological friction assessment) or Occam's Razor (for MVP simplification).

---

## Layer 2: Master's Mindset

**Chief Practitioners**: Elon Musk, Aristotle.

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Extreme Aversion to Analogical Thinking | Absolutely reject reasoning like "everyone else does it this way" or "it failed before, so it's impossible." |
| Seeking Physical Limits | The only baseline: "Does this violate the laws of physics?" If not, it is merely an engineering problem that *can* be solved. |
| Periodic Table Pricing | When judging costs, ignore market quotes. Look only at raw material spot prices (e.g., London Metal Exchange). The gap between market price and raw cost is the innovation space. |

---

## Layer 3: Step-by-Step Execution

**All three steps are mandatory. No skipping.**

### Step 1: Capture Assumptions

**Action**: List every limitation and premise that everyone "believes to be true" or "habitually accepts" regarding the current problem.

**Self-Prompt**: *What is our conventional understanding of this issue? What do people treat as given?*

> Document every assumption, no matter how obvious it seems. These are the targets for deconstruction.

---

### Step 2: Brute-Force Deconstruction

**Action**: Apply the Socratic method — ask "Why?" five consecutive times for every assumption from Step 1 — until you reach irrefutable foundational facts (mathematical laws, chemical compositions, physical limits).

**Self-Prompt**: *Is this limitation truly a fact? Or is it a compromise based on regulations, habits, or current technology? If stripped down completely, what are its most basic components?*

> **Critical rule**: Regulations, market norms, and industry practices are human-made assumptions — they can be lobbied or changed. Only natural science and mathematics qualify as "absolute facts."

---

### Step 3: Lego Recomposition

**Action**: Discard all original plans. Use only the "absolute facts" extracted in Step 2 as building blocks to construct a completely new path from scratch — one with lower costs or higher efficiency.

**Self-Prompt**: *If I had zero constraints except physics and math, what solution would I build?*

> The result must be a concrete, actionable execution path — not a philosophical observation.

## Layer 4: Output Specification

All outputs **must** strictly follow these three mandatory formats so the Orchestrator can chain them to the next Skill.

### Mandatory Format 1 — Assumptions vs. Reality Table

| Industry Norm / Common Assumption (Illusion) | Brute-Force Deconstructed Absolute Fact (Truth) |
|----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| e.g., Battery pack costs $600/kWh; too expensive | e.g., Raw metals (carbon, nickel, aluminum) cost only $80/kWh |
| *(fill with problem-specific assumptions)* | *(fill with verified absolute facts)* |

### Mandatory Format 2 — Actionable Solution

Propose **at least one specific, actionable execution path** built solely from the "Truth" column of the table above. The solution must be concrete enough to execute — no abstract principles.

### Mandatory Format 3 — Constraint Check

One sentence confirming the recomposed solution does not violate natural laws or mathematical logic.

> Example: *"This solution relies only on established electrochemical principles and does not require any physical law violations."*

## Layer 5: Pitfall Guide & Iteration Log

*(Read by the Orchestrator to prevent errors. Dynamically updated after each review.)*

### Known Blind Spot 1 — Analysis Paralysis

First Principles consumes massive cognitive resources. **Do not invoke this Skill for trivial, low-leverage decisions** (e.g., what tool to use for a minor task, lunch choices). Reserve it for high-stakes, high-leverage problems.

### Known Blind Spot 2 — Ignoring Social Friction

A physically viable solution may face massive sociological resistance (e.g., pushback from interest groups, regulatory inertia). **This Skill is only responsible for the optimal theoretical solution.** Human and organizational resistance must be handled downstream by [SWOT Analysis] or [Reverse Thinking / Broken Window].
